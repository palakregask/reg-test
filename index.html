Medicines and Medical Devices Regulations — MED/2019-32

Section 47 — Adverse Reaction Reporting Obligations

Current to 2026-04-01 · Last amended 2026-02-28 · Previous versions

Part 8, Division 3 — Pharmacovigilance Reporting

Note: This section applies to all holders of a marketing authorisation for a medicine or vaccine, and to all licensed manufacturers, wholesalers and compounders, as defined under section 4 of these Regulations. See also: Schedule 3 — Prescribed Forms and Data Elements for Adverse Reaction Reporting.

Obligation to Report Suspected Adverse Reactions

47 Every marketing authorisation holder must report to the Authority every suspected adverse reaction to a medicine or vaccine for which the holder is responsible, where the holder has become aware of the reaction in the course of its pharmacovigilance activities or by any other means.
(2) A report under subsection (1) must include
(a) an identifiable reporter, an identifiable patient, the suspected medicinal product, and the suspected reaction;
(b) the seriousness of the reaction, assessed against the criteria set out in Schedule 3, Part II;
(c) any concomitant medication, relevant medical history, and the outcome of the reaction at the time of reporting; and
(d) an assessment of causality using a methodology recognised by the World Health Organization or the Authority.
(3) A marketing authorisation holder who fails to submit a report within the time prescribed by section 48 is liable to an administrative penalty as set out in Schedule 6.

Time Limits for Reporting

48 A serious suspected adverse reaction occurring in Norvik must be reported to the Authority no later than 15 calendar days after the day on which the marketing authorisation holder became aware of the reaction.
(2) A non-serious suspected adverse reaction occurring in Norvik must be reported no later than 90 calendar days after the day on which the marketing authorisation holder became aware of the reaction.
(3) A serious suspected adverse reaction occurring outside Norvik must be reported within the same 15-day period, where the medicinal product to which the reaction relates is authorised in Norvik.

Format and Submission

49 Reports must be submitted electronically using the National Pharmacovigilance Database in the ICH E2B(R3) format, or in such other format as the Authority may approve in writing.
(2) Where technical difficulties prevent electronic submission, a marketing authorisation holder may submit a paper report on the prescribed form accompanied by a written explanation. The Authority may, at its discretion, accept or reject such a submission.
(3) A report submitted under this section is not, of itself, an admission that the medicinal product caused the reaction described.

Urgent Safety Restrictions

50.1 Where a marketing authorisation holder identifies a new safety signal that, in its reasonable opinion, requires urgent action to protect public health, the holder must immediately cease the distribution of the affected batch or product and notify the Authority by telephone within two hours of forming that opinion.
(2) Within 24 hours of the notification required under subsection (1), the marketing authorisation holder must submit a written safety report to the Authority containing
(a) the identity of the affected product, including the batch numbers, expiry dates, and quantities distributed in Norvik;
(b) the nature of the suspected safety signal and the data on which it is based;
(c) the immediate risk-minimisation measures taken or proposed, including any Dear Healthcare Professional communication; and
(d) a proposed timeline for resolving the safety signal or returning the product to the market.
(3) Distribution of the affected product may not resume until the Authority issues written clearance. The Authority must respond within 72 hours of receiving the safety report, failing which the holder may seek a determination from the Director-General.

Publication of Aggregate Data

50.2 The Authority must publish a summary of all serious adverse reaction reports received under section 47(1) on the National Medicines Regulatory Portal no later than September 30 of each year, in a format accessible to the general public.
(2) The published summary must include, for each medicinal product,
(a) the active substance, marketing authorisation number, and authorisation holder;
(b) the total number of serious adverse reaction reports received by reaction class; and
(c) whether any regulatory action was taken in respect of the product during the reporting period.
(3) A marketing authorisation holder may apply to the Authority to redact commercially sensitive information from the public summary. The Authority must render a decision within 45 days and may grant a redaction only where disclosure would identify an individual patient or reporter, or where it would cause demonstrable competitive harm that outweighs the public interest in transparency.

Protection of Healthcare Professionals and Patients

51 No marketing authorisation holder, manufacturer, or employer may dismiss, suspend, demote, or otherwise penalise a healthcare professional or other person who, in good faith, reports a suspected adverse reaction to the Authority.
(2) A person who believes they have been penalised in contravention of subsection (1) may file a complaint with the Health and Disability Commissioner within 60 days of the alleged reprisal.
(3) Upon receipt of a complaint under subsection (2), the burden of proof lies with the person against whom the complaint is made to demonstrate that any adverse action was unrelated to the report made under subsection (1).
